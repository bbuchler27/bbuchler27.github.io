The Truth Behind Playoff Hockey

Testing whether goals scored or goalie save percentage better explained NHL playoff qualification.

Comparison based on the project's reported AIC values. Lower AIC indicates better relative model fit.

The Analytical Question

Does goaltending explain NHL playoff qualification better than a team's ability to score goals? I tested a common hockey intuition using data rather than relying on conventional wisdom.

My Role & Methods

I completed this individual academic analysis in R, combining hockeyR play-by-play information with MoneyPuck goalie statistics across the 2021–22, 2022–23, and 2023–24 NHL seasons.

I prepared team-season data and developed logistic regression models to evaluate the relationship between offensive production, goaltending performance, and playoff qualification.

Key Findings

The goals-scored model had an AIC of 54.31, compared with 83.35 for the save-percentage model.

Because lower AIC indicates better relative model fit, goals scored provided the stronger explanation among the two models evaluated.

This result challenged my initial expectations and demonstrated the importance of testing assumptions through statistical analysis.

Challenges & Limitations

Combining multiple hockey datasets required aligning teams and seasons while ensuring consistency across observations.

The analysis covers only three seasons, and team-level goalie aggregates can conceal differences among individual goaltenders.

The models establish associations rather than causation. AIC measures relative model fit, not standalone predictive accuracy.

Business Value

Professional sports organizations make consequential decisions under uncertainty, including player acquisitions, roster construction, and resource allocation.

This project demonstrates my ability to evaluate competing explanations, compare statistical models, and communicate counterintuitive findings in a way that decision-makers can understand.
